\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Track 1 asks for the onset and offset of every non-speech noise event in Indian-language speech and scores event-level F1 plus segment-level Dice. We train frame-level taggers of six designs on 20\,ms frames. Each combines WavLM-Base+ (truncated to four layers, or all twelve with a learned layer mix) with an optional AudioSet-strong BEATs stream, a bidirectional GRU, and heads for frame presence, clip presence, signed distance to the nearest boundary and, optionally, latched start/end states. The test set mixes field recordings with synthetic mixtures, which a waveform-peak rule separates (exactly, on validation), so each population gets its own ensemble and decoder. A hysteresis readout with boundary refinement is followed by post-processing that models how annotators labelled the data. The final system scores 1.5926 on the leaderboard, sixth overall, with F1/Dice of 0.494/0.718 on natural clips and 0.993/0.985 on synthetic ones. No external data is used; all code, the six surviving checkpoints and all test posteriors are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Training Data and Preprocessing}
\textbf{Vaani dataset.} We use only the official Track 1 release of the VAANI noise-event dataset~\cite{vaani_ned}, which marks seven noise categories (animal, vehicle, baby, music, alarm, appliance, human non-speech) in speech from the VAANI corpus~\cite{vaani}. Events are short (median 0.48\,s on validation, 14\% under 250\,ms) and one validation clip in eight has overlapping events. The human-verified tier (\emph{Gold}, 11{,}111 clips, 21.85\,h) is the core training set. From the unverified tier we keep clips whose longest event lasts at least 1.5\,s (\emph{Silver}, 12{,}014 clips; a further unrecorded filter removed 216), down-weighted by 0.5 in the frame, clip and boundary losses. The weakly labelled tier is unused. We split the 2{,}501 validation clips by speaker key (SHA-256, seed 42) into TUNE (1{,}257 clips) and DECIDE (1{,}244), with no shared speaker keys. TUNE is used for training, as the noise bank below, and for tuning readout thresholds. Ensemble composition and every post-processing stage were accepted only if they improved DECIDE, on which only v5all (synthetic route) trains.

\textbf{Additional datasets.} None. No external dataset is used for training, tuning or augmentation; AudioSet~\cite{audioset,audioset_strong} enters only through the pretrained BEATs weights (Table~\ref{tab:pre}).

\textbf{Preprocessing.} Audio is read with \texttt{soundfile}, averaged to mono, resampled to 16\,kHz with \texttt{librosa}, and stored as int16. Labels are rasterised to 20\,ms frames as eight channels: the union of all events (the scored target) and one channel per category. Boundary targets give each frame the signed distance to its nearest onset and offset, clipped to $\pm0.5$\,s. Models see 5\,s crops, each z-normalised, drawn at random (bts\_R: event-centred with probability 0.5).

\textbf{Augmentation techniques and pipeline.} Two synthetic stores are built offline (crops are the only online augmentation). Both draw from a noise bank cut from the 363 TUNE synthetic clips, for which validation ships matched clean and noisy versions: the residual \texttt{noisy}$-$\texttt{clean} is taken over each labelled event $\pm$20\,ms, skipping events under 0.15\,s. Every bank event is resampled by a factor $\mathcal{U}(0.85,1.18)$, tilted by a one-pole filter with coefficient $\mathcal{U}(-0.6,0.6)$, and reversed with probability 0.35. In both stores the host keeps its own Gold labels, insertions are mixed at an SNR measured against the host RMS over the same span, and a clip whose peak exceeds 0.99 is rescaled to 0.99. \emph{synth\_aug} (9{,}982 clips, seed 0) uses Gold hosts of at least 3\,s, preferring those with the least labelled noise, and inserts 1--4 events at uniform positions and SNR $\mathcal{U}(-5,15)$\,dB. An insertion is skipped when its onset falls inside an existing event $\pm$50\,ms or when it is longer than 0.8$\times$ the host. \emph{synth\_mask\_t} (9{,}000 clips, seed 5) imitates the long events masked by speech that natural recordings contain. A bank event is looped with 50\,ms crossfades (each copy reversed with probability 0.5) to a length $\mathcal{U}(2,\min(8,0.85\,d_\text{host}))$\,s, discarded below 1.5\,s, mixed into a Gold host of at least 4\,s at $\mathcal{U}(-12,2)$\,dB, and labelled over its full extent.

\section{Proposed Method}
\textbf{Model architecture} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sys}a). Most designs keep the first four of the twelve layers of WavLM-Base+~\cite{wavlm}, with the convolutional front-end frozen; v12 keeps all twelve and learns a softmax mixture of hidden states, initialised at layer 4. The optional second stream is BEATs~\cite{beats} as fine-tuned on AudioSet-strong labels by PretrainedSED~\cite{schmid2025}. It supplies a 768-dimensional embedding plus 447 class logits per 40\,ms frame, upsampled to 20\,ms, and is either precomputed and frozen or fine-tuned inside the model. Both streams are layer-normalised and concatenated (1983-d), optionally passed through a two-layer Conformer~\cite{conformer} (256-d, 4 heads, kernel 31), and fed to a two-layer BiGRU~\cite{gru} with 256 units per direction. Up to four heads read the GRU states. The frame head outputs eight presence logits; the clip head attention-pools them into eight clip probabilities; the boundary head regresses signed seconds to the nearest onset and offset. The recurrent event detection (RED) head~\cite{schmid2026red} predicts, per channel, start and end probabilities $a_t,b_t$ and decodes presence as the latch $p_t=a_t(1-p_{t-1})+(1-b_t)\,p_{t-1}$, computed with a parallel scan. Where it exists, this latch replaces the frame sigmoid at inference.

\textbf{Loss formulations.} The objective is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_\text{frame}+0.3\,\mathcal{L}_\text{clip}+0.3\,\mathcal{L}_\text{bd}+\mathcal{L}_\text{RED}$. $\mathcal{L}_\text{frame}$ is a binary cross-entropy (BCE, positive weight 2) over all eight channels, $\mathcal{L}_\text{clip}$ a BCE against the crop maximum, and $\mathcal{L}_\text{bd}$ a Huber loss on (prediction$-$target)/50\,ms over frames within 0.5\,s of a boundary. For RED designs only,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{RED}=\mathcal{L}^\text{focal}_\text{pres}+100\,(\mathcal{L}_\text{on}+\mathcal{L}_\text{off}),
\end{equation}
a focal ($\gamma\!=\!2$)~\cite{focal} presence loss plus the onset/offset loss of~\cite{schmid2026red}, unweighted by tier. Clips whose events cover at least 95\% of the audio carry no frame contrast; they get weight 0 in all three losses, except in designs with whole-clip presence kept ($\dagger$ in Table~\ref{tab:mem}), which train on them normally.

\textbf{Training strategy.} We train in PyTorch~\cite{pytorch} with AdamW~\cite{adamw} (weight decay 0.05) with a one-cycle schedule~\cite{onecycle} (15\% warm-up). Learning rates are $5\times10^{-5}$ for WavLM and fine-tuned BEATs and $10^{-3}$ for the heads; in v5\_S and v12 the BEATs layers instead use layer-wise learning-rate decay (LLRD) 0.8~\cite{ulmfit} from $10^{-4}$, after one heads-only epoch. Batches are 6 crops with 3 accumulation steps, gradients are clipped at 5, and training runs in bf16 autocast. A checkpoint is chosen by average precision of the union channel minus the capped boundary error on an internal 10\% split grouped by state, district and language. Each checkpoint stores its full configuration (Table~\ref{tab:mem}).

\textbf{Implementation details: inference and readout} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sys}b). Posteriors are computed on 5\,s windows with a 2.5\,s hop and averaged where windows overlap. On the natural route three members also use test-time augmentation (TTA): the clip is zero-padded on both sides by 0, 0.64 and 1.28\,s, and the trimmed outputs are averaged. Natural (\nat) recordings peak at int16 full scale ($\geq$32{,}765), synthetic (\syn) mixtures at 0.99 of it; this rule is exact on validation and splits the test set into 3{,}437 natural and 2{,}080 synthetic clips. The readout (\texttt{asym} in the configs) is hysteresis: an event is a maximal run of frames with $p\!\geq\!0.6$ that contains at least one frame with $p\!\geq\!0.7$. Gaps of at most 60\,ms are merged, then events shorter than 0.2\,s (\nat) or 0.08\,s (\syn) are dropped. Each edge then moves to the median of the positions proposed by the boundary head over all frames within $\pm$0.2\,s (\nat) or $\pm$0.3\,s (\syn); moves over 0.35\,s are rejected. On natural clips, runs of the human-non-speech channel ($\geq$0.55, $\geq$60\,ms) inside a predicted event at least 4$\times$ longer are added as extra events. A clip left empty receives one event over the frames above 0.7 of its maximum posterior.

\textbf{Implementation details: annotation-aware post-pass.} The natural-clip stages follow labelling habits measured on validation. \emph{Background extension} lengthens events of at least 1\,s in the animal, singing/music and appliance categories while the union posterior stays above 0.3, and bridges gaps of at most 0.5\,s between two events of at least 1\,s under the same condition. The \emph{habit} stage adds one clip-length event (keeping the others) when no event covers 90\% of the clip and a classifier gives $p\geq0.5$ that annotators marked one event other than human non-speech over at least half of it. The classifier, a histogram gradient-boosted model~\cite{sklearn} trained on Gold and natural TUNE clips, sees BEATs-strong tags pooled into nine sound groups, the duration, and the recording state parsed from the clip id (DECIDE AUC 0.73). \emph{Nested events} (``type-C'' in the code) add a category run (score $\geq$0.65 for $\geq$0.3\,s, gaps $\leq$0.12\,s bridged, IoU $<$0.5 with existing events) inside a predicted event at least twice as long whose dominant category differs. The category-agnostic scorer can still match such nested events. On synthetic clips we drop events quieter than $-16$\,dB relative to the clip and shift edges by +8\,ms (onset) and $-6$\,ms (offset).

\section{Results and Conclusion}
\textbf{Evaluation metrics.} Track 1 is category-agnostic: only time spans count, and the score is $S=F_1+\mathrm{Dice}$. $F_1$ is event-based~\cite{mesaros2016} and micro-pooled over all clips with greedy one-to-one matching; the organisers' scorer accepts a match when both onset and offset lie within $\max(0.2\,d_\text{ref},50\,\text{ms})$ of the reference, where $d_\text{ref}$ is its duration. Dice is computed per clip on a 10\,ms grid and averaged over clips.

\textbf{Results.} Each of the five submissions (Table~\ref{tab:lb}) raised the score, the habit stage most (+0.0050, natural only); the last scores 1.5926 (rank 6).

\textbf{Key findings.} The natural route is the bottleneck: synthetic clips are near ceiling, natural F1 is 0.494. Fig.~\ref{fig:wf} shows why natural references are missed on DECIDE: mostly merged neighbours and misplaced edges; 18\% are not detected at all. Leaderboard gains were about 0.46$\times$ the DECIDE estimates, so DECIDE is optimistic. Members on stronger encoders (WavLM-Large with BEATs; WavLM with Dasheng~\cite{dasheng}) gave the best single models on DECIDE but lowered the ensemble by 0.008--0.010. Natural edge shifts ($-$0.0015), an edge-correction CNN ($-$0.0019), duplicated events ($-$0.0035) and TTA on bts\_C ($-$0.0038) also lost on DECIDE and were not submitted.

Rebuilt from the cached posteriors, the final file matches the upload at official-scorer F1 0.9996 / Dice 0.9993. Three members were lost with a cloud instance; live inference with substitutes (Table~\ref{tab:pre}) agrees at F1 0.984 / Dice 0.972, taking 66\,min for the 10.8\,h test set on a laptop RTX 4070.

\textbf{Conclusion.} A WavLM ensemble with explicit boundary supervision, decoded per route and post-processed to match how annotators label, reached 1.5926 and rank 6 without external data. Separating merged neighbours and placing natural edges remain the open problems.
